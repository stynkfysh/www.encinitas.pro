Prop 19 and Property Tax Appraisals for Encinitas Homeowners and Heirs

Market value reports for Prop 19 parent-child transfers, assessment appeals and base year questions brought before the San Diego County Assessor.

Property tax appraisals help owners and heirs deal with the San Diego County Assessor when a transfer or reassessment changes what they owe. Under Proposition 19, in effect since February 2021, a child who inherits or is given a parent's home retains the low taxable value of the parent only when the child moves in and makes it a primary residence within a year, and only up to a cap equal to $1 million added to the parent's factored base year value, a figure periodically adjusted for inflation. Any market value exceeding that cap gets added to the assessment. So the crucial figure becomes what the home was worth on the date of transfer. Families, estate attorneys and tax preparers order these appraisals to estimate the new assessment before deciding whether a child should move in, and to support or question the assessor's value once a reassessment notice arrives.

When this report is needed

  • A child inherits a parent's Encinitas home and plans to live there
  • Heirs must estimate the new assessment before deciding to keep or sell
  • The assessor's transfer value seems too high and an appeal is being considered
  • A family wants transfer-date market value to accompany a BOE-19-P claim
  • Value above the exclusion amount is reassessed and the figure is disputed
  • A grandparent-to-grandchild transfer qualifies because the grandchild's parents are deceased
  • Falling values justify a Proposition 8 review of the current assessment

How Brian handles it

  1. Identify the transfer date

    In Prop 19 assignments, the effective date is generally the date of death or the date the home was transferred by deed. Brian confirms that date with the family or attorney, since the assessor measures market value when ownership changes, not on the date of a later trust distribution or sale.

  2. Review the assessor's record

    The current factored base year value, recorded square footage and assessed improvements are pulled from county records. Errors do occur, such as a missing bathroom or an addition counted twice, and correcting them can matter as much as the value conclusion itself.

  3. Estimate transfer-date value

    Comparable sales around the transfer date establish market value. The report sets that figure beside the factored base year value and the exclusion amount, so the family and its tax adviser can estimate how much would be added to the taxable value if the child keeps the house.

  4. Support the claim or appeal

    The report can accompany a BOE-19-P claim, a request for informal review or a formal application to the county assessment appeals board. If the matter goes to hearing, Brian can help counsel understand the evidence the board expects and, under a separate arrangement, appear as a witness.

The coastal angle

Prop 19 hits coastal Encinitas especially hard because so many homes have stayed with one owner for decades. A Cardiff or Leucadia house bought long ago may carry a taxable value that is a small fraction of its market value, and the gap between the two can exceed the exclusion amount. In those cases the transfer-date appraisal directly shapes the child's new tax bill. Bluff homes, view lots and properties with ADUs are the hardest to value because a handful of recent sales can swing the estimate. Olivenhain acreage raises separate questions about how land, outbuildings and any agricultural use are treated. A careful appraisal gives families a realistic picture before they commit to moving in or decide that selling makes more sense.

Desktop or inspected?

A desktop report is often enough for planning, such as estimating the likely reassessment before a child decides whether to move into an inherited home. Desktop reports are available for homes anywhere in California. For a formal assessment appeal, an inspected report carries more weight because the appeals board sees firsthand evidence of condition, quality and any features the assessor's data missed. Inspected reports are limited to Brian's in-person service area, which includes Encinitas and neighboring North County communities. When the transfer date is well in the past, a retrospective desktop report may be the practical choice either way.

A site visit is part of the offer only where Brian works on the ground: 68 communities, 33 of them in San Diego County (Encinitas is one) and 35 in Riverside County. Drive-By and Standard reports, single-family or 2-4 unit, are booked for those addresses alone. Any other California property is valued with one of the three desktop formats.

Questions

Does Prop 19 still let parents pass their low property tax to children?

Only in limited form. For any transfer dated February 16, 2021 or later, the parent-child exclusion covers a family home the child occupies as a primary residence within one year, protecting the parent's factored base year value plus $1 million adjusted for inflation. Value above that is added to the taxable amount. Other property, such as a rental or second home, is reassessed at market value when transferred. The family files a claim with the county assessor, generally on form BOE-19-P.

How is the new assessment calculated when value exceeds the limit?

Put simply, the assessor weighs the home's market value on the date of transfer against the sum of the exclusion amount and the parent's factored base year value. If market value is higher, the excess is added to the factored base year value to create the new taxable value. The exact calculation should be confirmed with the assessor or a tax professional, but an accurate market value is the input that determines how large the increase will be.

Can an appraisal be used to appeal the assessor's value?

Yes. If a reassessment notice shows a market value that seems too high, the owner can first ask the assessor for an informal review and, if needed, file an application with the assessment appeals board within the filing period that applies to the notice. The appraisal provides evidence of market value on the relevant date. Appeals succeed or fail on the quality of that evidence, so well-chosen comparable sales and clear condition documentation matter more than opinion.
