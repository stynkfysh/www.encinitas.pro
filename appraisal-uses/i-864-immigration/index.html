Appraisal use

Form I-864 Home Equity Appraisals for Immigration Sponsors

A recent appraisal by a licensed appraiser documenting home value so net equity can be counted on an Affidavit of Support filed with USCIS.

This type of appraisal establishes what a sponsor's house is worth, allowing its equity to count as an asset on the USCIS Affidavit of Support. A sponsor whose household earnings are under 125 percent of federal poverty guidelines can cover the gap with assets, and home equity is frequently the biggest one on hand. The USCIS instructions require proof of value; for a house, that means a current appraisal from a licensed appraiser, plus documentation of ownership and of any liens. Net equity is the appraised value minus the balances owed on liens against the property. Sponsors, joint sponsors and immigration attorneys order this appraisal when preparing a family-based petition or responding to a request for evidence about the sponsor's assets.

When this report is needed

  • A sponsor's income falls short of the required level for the household size
  • A joint sponsor plans to use home equity to qualify
  • USCIS has issued a request for evidence about the sponsor's assets
  • An immigration attorney needs current value documentation before filing
  • Equity in a rental property will be listed as a sponsor asset
  • A household member's property will help meet the asset requirement under a signed household member contract

How Brian handles it

  1. Confirm filing needs

    Brian asks whether the appraisal will accompany an initial filing or answer a request for evidence, and who the sponsor is. The report identifies the owner of record, which must match the sponsor or household member whose assets are being counted on the affidavit.

  2. Value the property

    Comparable sales establish current market value. Since USCIS wants a current appraisal, the effective date stays near the filing date, and a fresh report can make sense if a request for evidence comes many months afterward.

  3. Present equity clearly

    The report states the appraised value clearly, and the sponsor supplies statements showing balances owed on any liens. That combination lets the attorney or USCIS calculate net equity by subtracting what is owed from the appraised value.

  4. Deliver a filing-ready report

    The completed appraisal arrives as a PDF bearing the appraiser's signature and license details, so it can go straight into the affidavit packet. Attorneys can receive a copy directly, and Brian answers questions if the valuation is questioned during the case.

The coastal angle

Encinitas homes often carry enough equity to cover a sponsor's entire shortfall, which is why the appraisal can be the decisive document in an asset-based Affidavit of Support. Long-held properties in Leucadia, Cardiff or Old Encinitas may hold far more equity than the sponsor realizes, while recent purchases may show less once liens are subtracted. Properties with ADUs present an added consideration: the unit contributes market value to the parcel, while any rent it produces is reported separately on the income side of the form. Olivenhain parcels with acreage may need extra explanation of land value. A clear appraisal that addresses these features helps an attorney present the asset with confidence.

Desktop or inspected?

USCIS wants a current appraisal from a licensed appraiser yet never states that an inspection is required, so a desktop report frequently suffices, especially when the equity is well above the needed figure. Desktop reports are available for homes anywhere in California. An inspected report adds weight when equity is close to the threshold, when the property has unusual features or when the attorney expects scrutiny of the assets. Inspected reports are limited to Brian's in-person service area, so sponsors elsewhere in the state typically use a desktop report.

A site visit is part of the offer only where Brian works on the ground: 68 communities, 33 of them in San Diego County (Encinitas is one) and 35 in Riverside County. Drive-By and Standard reports, single-family or 2-4 unit, are booked for those addresses alone. Any other California property is valued with one of the three desktop formats.

Questions

How much home equity does a Form I-864 sponsor need?

If earnings come up short, the sponsor typically needs assets worth five or more times the gap between household income and the income threshold. The multiplier drops to three times when the sponsor is a U.S. citizen petitioning for a spouse, and to one times for certain orphans adopted by U.S. citizens. Net home equity counts toward that total, so the appraised value directly affects whether the sponsor qualifies on assets.

What does a recent appraisal by a licensed appraiser mean?

USCIS does not set a strict age limit, but an appraisal completed close to the filing date is the safest approach, since an older report may not reflect current value. The appraiser must hold a valid state license or certification for the state where the property is located. As a California Certified Residential Real Estate Appraiser, Brian meets the license requirement stated in the instructions for any California property.

Can a property outside California be appraised for an I-864?

Brian's certification covers properties located in California, including homes anywhere in the state through desktop reports. A sponsor whose property is in another state needs an appraiser licensed in that state. A sponsor living outside California who owns a home or rental here can still order the appraisal from Brian, since the license requirement follows the property's location rather than where the sponsor lives.
