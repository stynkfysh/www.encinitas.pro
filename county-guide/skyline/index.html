Central San Diego

Skyline home appraisals

Ridge-top postwar neighborhoods between Encanto and Spring Valley

Located on hills in southeastern San Diego, Skyline lies east of Interstate 805 and north of Paradise Hills, adjoining Spring Valley and Lemon Grove on its eastern side. It takes its name from Skyline Drive, which runs along the ridges. Most of the area was subdivided and built in the late 1950s and 1960s, as postwar demand pushed tract construction onto the hillsides. Together with Paradise Hills, Bay Terraces, Jamacha and Lomita, it forms the Skyline-Paradise Hills community planning area.

In-person service. Skyline falls inside Brian's visit footprint, so all six formats, Drive-By and Standard included, can be ordered for a property here.

Housing in Skyline

Most of the housing supply consists of detached single-family homes dating from the late 1950s through the 1970s. Single-story ranch homes with three or four bedrooms, attached two-car garages and lots of 5,000 to 8,000 square feet line streets in Skyline Hills, Jamacha and Lomita. Split-level and two-story homes are common on slopes, many with views. Later construction from the 1980s and 1990s includes townhome and condominium complexes and some larger detached homes in the Bay Terraces area. Garage conversions, room additions and new ADUs are widespread. Some streets back onto open canyons that provide privacy and green outlooks, while others face busy Skyline Drive or Paradise Valley Road.

What shifts value here

Elevation and outlook are key factors, with ridge lots looking west toward the bay or east toward the mountains outperforming flat interior streets. Homes backing onto canyons gain privacy but may face fire and slope considerations. Traffic on Skyline Drive and Paradise Valley Road affects frontage homes. Many houses retain original systems that buyers expect to replace, so updated kitchens, roofs and electrical work have visible effects on price. Condominium and townhome values depend partly on HOA fees and reserves. Skyline offers relatively affordable detached homes within city limits, attracting first-time buyers, which supports steady demand for well-maintained properties.

Valuation approach

Comparable sales are selected from Skyline Hills, Jamacha, Lomita and Bay Terraces, with nearby Spring Valley and Lemon Grove sales considered when inventory is limited. Adjustments address view, canyon frontage, traffic exposure, gross living area including additions, and condition. Owners should gather permits for room additions and garage conversions, documentation of roof, HVAC and electrical upgrades, any ADU approvals, and HOA documents for attached units. Records of slope stabilization, drainage repairs or brush clearance are especially helpful for homes that back onto open canyon land.

Local question

Will an appraiser treat my converted garage in Skyline as living area?

Only if it was permitted and is finished to a standard similar to the rest of the house. A permitted conversion with heating, proper flooring and egress can be counted as living area. An unpermitted conversion is usually described separately and given a lower contribution. The appraisal also considers that converting the garage removes covered parking, which some buyers value.
