Nestor home appraisals

Tijuana River valley edge with older homes and quiet streets

Bordering San Ysidro and Imperial Beach, Nestor is a City of San Diego community located west of Interstate 805 and south of the Otay River. The area began as farmland and small ranches in the Tijuana River valley, with early settlements near Palm City. The City of San Diego annexed the area in the late 1950s, and postwar subdivisions filled the flatlands with modest homes. Nestor retains a semi-rural feel near the river valley, where horse properties and open space remain.

In-person service. Nestor falls inside Brian's visit footprint, so all six formats, Drive-By and Standard included, can be ordered for a property here.

Housing in Nestor

Housing in Nestor centers on single-family homes from the 1950s through the 1970s, often on lots larger than those in much of South San Diego. Some older homes sit on large parcels near the Tijuana River valley with room for horses or workshops. Egger Highlands and Palm City add tract homes, duplexes, and small apartment buildings on a grid of streets near Coronado Avenue and Palm Avenue, while newer townhomes and condos have appeared near Palm Avenue and the trolley. Garage conversions and ADUs are common, and some properties include multiple units, particularly on the deeper lots that date from the area's farming years.

What shifts value here

Lot size, condition, and ADU potential drive value, along with proximity to the Tijuana River Valley Regional Park and open space. Homes near Interstate 5, Interstate 805, or Palm Avenue face traffic noise. Low-lying areas near the river face flood considerations, and pollution in the Tijuana River can affect appeal. Homes on the higher ground near Coronado Avenue avoid most flood concerns, and some have views toward the ocean or the Otay Valley. The Blue Line trolley stop at Palm Avenue adds convenience for commuters. Investor demand for rental properties influences pricing, and equestrian properties near the valley attract a specific group of buyers.

Valuation approach

An appraiser draws comparables from Nestor and nearby South San Diego communities, adjusting for lot size, condition, ADUs, and proximity to freeways or the river valley. Horse properties may require comparisons with similar parcels in the valley or the rural edges of Bonita and Otay Mesa, adjusting for location. Flood zone status should be confirmed on FEMA maps, since it affects both insurance and buyer interest. Unpermitted additions on older homes are reviewed separately from original living area. Owners should provide permits for additions or ADUs, rental information, and flood zone documentation.

Local question

Are larger lots near the Tijuana River valley valued differently in Nestor?

Larger lots near the valley can appeal to buyers seeking horses or space, but flood risk and pollution concerns may affect demand. The appraiser compares sales of similar parcels in Nestor, Palm City, and the Tijuana River valley, adjusting for lot size, usable land, and flood zone status, and documents the factors in the report.
