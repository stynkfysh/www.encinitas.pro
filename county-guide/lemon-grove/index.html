Lemon Grove home appraisals

Small city of flat streets, older cottages and a giant lemon

Covering about four square miles, Lemon Grove is a small city located between Spring Valley, La Mesa and southeastern San Diego. Citrus orchards gave the place its name in the late 1800s, and a large lemon sculpture on Broadway near the trolley tracks still marks the town center. Residential subdivision replaced the groves through the first half of the twentieth century, and the community incorporated as a city in 1977. The city is bounded by State Route 125 and State Route 94, and the Orange Line trolley passes through its center.

Housing in Lemon Grove

Most of the housing stock is detached single-family homes on relatively flat lots, many larger than the typical San Diego city lot because they were carved from former orchard parcels. Small prewar cottages and 1940s houses sit near Broadway and the older streets around the town center, while 1950s and 1960s ranch homes fill most of the residential blocks. Some properties along the southern and eastern edges climb into hillier terrain with modest views. Apartment buildings, plus fewer condominiums, are grouped near the trolley stations and along Lemon Grove Avenue and Broadway. The generous lot sizes have made Lemon Grove a common location for detached ADUs and second units, and some larger parcels still have room for additional structures.

What shifts value here

Lot size and usability are major value drivers in Lemon Grove, since many parcels have space for an ADU, a workshop or parking for recreational vehicles. Homes close to State Route 94 or State Route 125 can be affected by traffic noise, as can properties along the busier commercial streets and the trolley line. Condition varies widely: many original postwar houses still have older electrical, plumbing and single-pane windows, while flipped homes in the same tract show full modern remodels. Hillier lots near the southern edge with outlooks toward the valley can carry a premium. The town's relative affordability compared with La Mesa keeps demand steady from buyers priced out of nearby areas.

Valuation approach

Lemon Grove sales should be compared closely on lot size, since the gap between a standard lot and an oversized former orchard parcel can be significant. An appraiser will also separate fully remodeled homes from original-condition houses, as the two groups trade at very different levels. Sales from Spring Valley or La Mesa may be needed when local data is thin, with adjustments for location. Owners should document permitted ADUs, room additions, solar systems and any recent replacement of the sewer lateral, roof or windows. Notes on unpermitted garage conversions help the appraiser treat that space correctly.

Local question

How does a detached ADU on a Lemon Grove lot affect value for an estate or trust appraisal?

A permitted detached ADU usually adds value in Lemon Grove because it creates rental income potential or space for extended family. The appraiser looks for nearby sales with similar second units and compares them with single-dwelling sales on comparable lots. An unpermitted unit is typically given less weight. Permits, final inspection records and any rental history help support the contribution of the ADU in the report.
