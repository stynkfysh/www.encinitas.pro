Coast and Mesas

Bay Ho home appraisals

Mid-century mesa streets above Rose Canyon and Morena Boulevard

Bay Ho is a compact residential enclave on the northwest corner of the Clairemont mesa, roughly between Morena Boulevard and Moraga Avenue near Balboa Avenue. It began as a suburban subdivision in 1954 during the same wave that produced greater Clairemont, and its streets were laid across gently sloping land overlooking Mission Bay and the Rose Canyon corridor. The Stonehaven development, a private community with ponds, added attached homes in the 1970s. Commercial frontage along Morena Boulevard, including the site where the first Price Club opened in 1976, borders the residential blocks.

Housing in Bay Ho

The core of Bay Ho is single-story tract housing from the mid and late 1950s, generally three-bedroom stucco homes of 1,100 to 1,600 square feet on lots around 6,000 square feet. Some sit on the western slope with partial views of Mission Bay and Mount Soledad, and many have been enlarged or updated with open floor plans. Stonehaven contributes townhomes and condominiums arranged around common open space and water features, with HOA maintenance. Apartment buildings appear near the commercial edges. Detached garages converted to ADUs or living space are found on a number of lots, and permit status varies.

What shifts value here

Bay Ho values rest on its central location between the beaches, UC San Diego and downtown, with easy access to Interstate 5 and the Balboa Avenue trolley station. Westward views toward the bay add a premium on upper slope lots, while homes closest to the freeway and Morena Boulevard commercial uses face traffic noise. Since the homes share similar ages and layouts, most of the variation in price comes from condition and the quality of any remodeling. Stonehaven prices hinge on unit size, HOA fees and the upkeep of common amenities. Lots backing onto Rose Canyon open space trade with a privacy premium.

Valuation approach

With a consistent 1950s housing stock, Bay Ho comparables are usually plentiful within the neighborhood and adjoining Clairemont tracts. The analysis focuses on condition, quality of updates, added square footage and view. Owners should list renovation dates for kitchens, baths, roof, windows, electrical and plumbing, and provide permits for additions or garage conversions. For a Stonehaven unit, the HOA budget, dues, reserve study and any special assessments are needed. Photos showing the original or updated condition at the effective date strengthen an estate or trust report.

Local question

Our Bay Ho house has a converted garage. Does the appraisal count it as living area?

The answer turns on whether the converted space had permits and was completed to the main home's standard, including heating and direct entry. Permitted, well-finished conversions are generally included in living area or treated as an ADU. Unpermitted conversions are usually reported separately and given a lower contribution, since a buyer may face costs to legalize or reverse them. Providing permit records helps the appraisal treat the space accurately.
