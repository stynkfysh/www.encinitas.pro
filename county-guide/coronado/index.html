South Bay

Coronado home appraisals

Island-style village of historic cottages, the Hotel del, and the Cays

Set on a peninsula facing downtown San Diego across the bay, Coronado links to the mainland via the Silver Strand and, starting in 1969, the San Diego-Coronado Bridge. Elisha Babcock and Hampton Story bought the land in 1885, sold lots through a heavily promoted auction, and opened the Hotel del Coronado in 1888. Naval Air Station North Island grew on the northern end through the twentieth century, while the village filled with homes on a grid of tree-lined streets, and the Coronado Cays added a waterfront enclave to the south in the 1970s.

Housing in Coronado

The central village holds an unusual range of architecture on small, often narrow lots: Victorian and Craftsman homes from the founding decades, Spanish Revival and Tudor cottages from the 1920s and 1930s, mid-century homes, and modern rebuilds. Ocean Boulevard and the streets near Glorietta Bay carry large custom estates. At the village's south end, Coronado Shores is made up of 1970s high-rise condominium towers that stand right on the sand. The Coronado Cays offer townhomes and detached homes along canals, many with private boat docks. Duplexes and small apartment buildings are scattered through the village, and companion units are found on some lots.

What shifts value here

Proximity to the beach, views of the ocean or bay, and lot size drive value, with Ocean Boulevard and bayfront properties commanding the highest prices. Coronado's historic preservation program and design standards affect what owners can change, and some homes carry historic designation with possible Mills Act tax benefits. Coronado Shores condos depend on building age, HOA dues, and ocean exposure, while Cays homes are judged on dock size and water frontage. Jet noise from North Island, bridge and Orange Avenue traffic, and flood or sea level exposure on low-lying streets also play a role, and military relocation cycles affect rental demand.

Valuation approach

Coronado is generally treated as its own market, so comparables come from the island itself, separated by village, Shores, and Cays. In the village, adjustments focus on lot size, distance to the beach, architectural style, condition, and historic status, and land value often makes up most of the price. Shores condos are compared within the same tower or similar towers, adjusting for floor level and view. Owners should provide permits, any historic designation or Mills Act contract, HOA documents, and dock or slip records for Cays homes.

Local question

Does historic designation help or hurt the value of a Coronado cottage?

It can work both ways. A historic designation can come with a Mills Act contract that reduces property taxes and attracts buyers who prize original design, though it restricts demolition and certain exterior alterations. To gauge buyer reaction, the appraiser seeks Coronado sales of both designated and non-designated houses, then records the tax savings and restrictions in the report, a helpful step for trust and estate assignments.
