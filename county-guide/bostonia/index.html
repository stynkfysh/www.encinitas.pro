Bostonia home appraisals

Older unincorporated neighborhoods on El Cajon's northeast edge

Bostonia is an unincorporated community on the northeastern edge of El Cajon, roughly bounded by Broadway, Greenfield Drive and the hills that rise toward Crest. The name comes from early settlers from the Boston area who established vineyards and orchards here in the 1880s. Agriculture continued until the postwar housing boom brought subdivisions across the former farmland. Today Bostonia functions much like a neighborhood of El Cajon, though it remains under County jurisdiction, with Interstate 8 just to the south and Gillespie Field a short drive west.

In-person service. Bostonia falls inside Brian's visit footprint, so all six formats, Drive-By and Standard included, can be ordered for a property here.

Housing in Bostonia

Most of Bostonia is one-story ranch houses built in the 1950s and 1960s, often on lots bigger than those in more recent subdivisions. Some streets retain the deep, narrow lots of former orchard parcels, offering room for RV parking, workshops or second units. Mobile home parks account for a considerable share of local housing, especially on Second Street and Broadway. Apartments and small multifamily buildings cluster near the commercial corridors. Toward the eastern hills, homes sit on sloped lots with some valley views. Many houses have been expanded with room additions or garage conversions, and ADUs are increasingly common. Older homes may still have original wiring, plumbing and windows.

What shifts value here

Lot size and usability play a large role in Bostonia values, since buyers often seek space for vehicles, outbuildings or ADUs. Homes on busy corridors like Broadway or near commercial areas may sell for less due to traffic and noise. Aircraft noise from Gillespie Field can affect some western portions. Condition varies widely, from original homes needing updates to fully remodeled properties. Hillside homes on the eastern edge with views can command premiums. As an unincorporated area, County permit records govern additions, and unpermitted work is a frequent issue. Bostonia generally offers lower prices than nearby Granite Hills or Fletcher Hills, attracting value-conscious buyers.

Valuation approach

Bostonia comparables often come from the community itself and from adjacent El Cajon neighborhoods with similar postwar housing. Adjustments typically address lot size, condition, garage or RV parking, and permitted versus unpermitted living area. The appraiser may also consider proximity to Gillespie Field or busy streets. Owners should gather County permits for additions, records of major system upgrades, and documentation of any ADUs or second units. A list of improvements with approximate dates helps the appraiser assess condition and updating.

Local question

Does being in unincorporated Bostonia rather than the city of El Cajon affect value?

Generally, buyers focus more on the house, lot and location than on jurisdiction. In Bostonia, the main practical difference is that permits and zoning run through the County of San Diego rather than the city. The appraiser typically uses sales from both Bostonia and nearby El Cajon neighborhoods, adjusting for location and property differences rather than jurisdiction alone.
