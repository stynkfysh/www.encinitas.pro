Allied Gardens home appraisals

1950s ranch homes between Mission Gorge and Lake Murray

Allied Gardens sits on the gently sloping mesa north of Interstate 8 and the San Diego River, between Mission Gorge Road and the hills that rise toward Lake Murray. It takes its name from Allied Contractors, the group of builders who began developing the area in the mid-1950s on former ranch land along the river. Within a few years the tract filled with modest single-family homes, a community park and neighborhood shopping. Allied Gardens forms part of the Navajo community planning area with Grantville, Del Cerro and San Carlos, and Mission Trails Regional Park lies just to the northeast.

In-person service. Allied Gardens falls inside Brian's visit footprint, so all six formats, Drive-By and Standard included, can be ordered for a property here.

Housing in Allied Gardens

Nearly all of the homes are one-story ranch houses dating from the middle to late 1950s, generally three or four bedrooms and 1,100 to 1,600 square feet, set on parcels sized between 6,000 and 7,500 square feet. Many have been remodeled, and a smaller number enlarged with second stories. Homes on the upper slopes along the eastern edge pick up views of the surrounding hills. Condominiums and apartments are concentrated near Mission Gorge Road and Grantville, where newer transit-oriented projects have been built close to the trolley. ADUs have become common in backyards across the tract.

What shifts value here

Allied Gardens values reflect its location between SDSU, Mission Valley and Mission Trails, along with relatively flat, usable lots. Homes on quiet interior streets sell above those on Waring Road or Mission Gorge Road, where traffic is heavier. Views over Mission Valley and the hills add value on higher lots. Because the homes are uniform in age and design, the level of renovation, bath count and added square footage drive most price differences. Proximity to the Grantville trolley station and redevelopment along Mission Gorge Road influence the market for multi-family and attached properties.

Valuation approach

Comparables for Allied Gardens homes are usually found within the original tract and adjacent parts of Del Cerro and San Carlos, matching size and era. Adjustments focus on condition, bath count, room additions, ADUs, pools and view. Owners should gather permits for additions, ADU documentation, dates of major updates such as roof, windows, electrical panel and plumbing, and photographs of the interior. For valuations as of a past date, records showing the home's condition at that time help support the condition rating.

Local question

How do appraisers treat a remodeled Allied Gardens home compared with an original one on the same street?

Remodeled and original homes are both common in Allied Gardens, so the appraisal can usually find sales of each. The subject is compared with homes of similar condition where possible. If not, an adjustment for condition and quality of updates is supported by comparing remodeled and unremodeled sales of similar size. Permits and remodel dates help document the improvements accurately.
