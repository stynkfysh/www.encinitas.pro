Backcountry

Boulevard home appraisals

High-desert ranch lands along Old Highway 80 near the wind ridges

In San Diego County's far southeastern corner, Boulevard is a rural community stretched along Interstate 8 and Old Highway 80 at about 3,300 to 4,000 feet. The area includes several small settlements, among them Live Oak Springs, Tierra del Sol, Manzanita and Bankhead Springs, each with its own history tied to the old highway. Ranchers and homesteaders settled the high plateau in the early twentieth century, and roadside resorts served motorists before the freeway bypassed them. Turbines on the surrounding ridges, such as those in McCain Valley and on the Campo Reservation, now stand out as a prominent feature of the scenery.

Desktop formats only. Boulevard lies past the edge of the 68 communities Brian visits, so a property here is valued from records, sale data and owner-supplied photos in one of the three desktop formats. Curbside and walk-through visit formats cannot be booked for this location.

Housing in Boulevard

Boulevard housing consists of detached homes on rural parcels. Manufactured homes and owner-built houses are common, often on lots of one to forty acres, along with ranch houses from the mid twentieth century. Live Oak Springs contains older cabins and small homes from the community's resort era. More recent custom houses sit on scattered lots with views, and certain properties run partially or entirely off grid using solar energy. Barns, workshops, water tanks and storage containers are standard on larger parcels. No condominiums, apartments or tract developments exist, and housing types vary widely across the area's many dispersed settlements.

What shifts value here

Boulevard is one of the most affordable parts of San Diego County, reflecting its distance from employment centers and services. Buyers seek space, privacy and lower land costs. Water comes from wells, and groundwater availability varies considerably, which is a central concern for buyers. Septic systems are standard. Wildfire risk and wind exposure influence insurance and maintenance. Proximity to wind energy projects and transmission lines affects views and buyer perception, and opinions about them vary. Interstate 8 access helps marketability. Homes on rough dirt roads, older manufactured units and parcels with weak wells are harder to sell, while newer construction with reliable water attracts stronger interest.

Valuation approach

An appraiser working in Boulevard usually separates small-lot older cabins in Live Oak Springs from acreage properties elsewhere. Comparables often include Campo, Jacumba Hot Springs and other remote eastern areas, with adjustments for water, power source, outbuildings, access, view and proximity to wind turbines or transmission lines. Owners should provide well production records, septic records, permits, solar system documents, manufactured home title or conversion records and information on private road maintenance agreements. Photographs of outbuildings and infrastructure help document the property.

Local question

Do the wind turbines near Boulevard lower home values?

Their effect varies by distance, visibility and buyer preference. Some buyers object to turbine views, noise or blinking lights, while others accept them in exchange for lower prices and open land. The appraiser looks for local sales near and away from turbines to see whether buyers have paid less. If the data shows a measurable difference, an adjustment is applied and explained. Otherwise the report notes the turbines as a neighborhood characteristic.
