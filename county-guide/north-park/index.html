Central San Diego

North Park home appraisals

Craftsman bungalows and walkable commercial streets east of Balboa Park

Centered where 30th Street crosses University Avenue, North Park covers the mesa to the northeast of Balboa Park. Developers subdivided former citrus groves here in the early 1900s, and the arrival of streetcar lines in the 1910s made the neighborhood one of San Diego's early suburbs. Most lots were built out between 1910 and the early 1940s, leaving a dense collection of Craftsman and Spanish Revival homes. After decades as a modest working neighborhood, North Park drew renewed interest beginning in the 2000s as restaurants, breweries and the restored North Park Theatre revived its commercial corridors.

Housing in North Park

Detached single-family homes on lots of around 5,000 to 6,250 square feet dominate the residential streets, with Craftsman bungalows the signature style and Spanish Revival, Prairie and minimal traditional houses filling in. Many homes measure between 900 and 1,500 square feet with two bedrooms, and owners have often added bedrooms, finished attics or rear additions. North Park also contains many two- to four-unit properties, courtyard apartments from the 1920s through 1960s, and condominium conversions. Several areas, including the Burlingame and Dryden historic districts, protect original architecture. Rear-yard ADUs have become common since state and city rules eased in 2017, and alley access on many blocks makes them easier to build.

What shifts value here

Walkability to 30th Street and University Avenue, the condition of original architectural features and the presence of a legal second unit are leading value factors. Homes in historic districts or with Mills Act contracts often sell for more because buyers value the preserved character and tax savings. Properties near Interstate 805 or State Route 15 absorb traffic noise, and those along busy corridors such as El Cajon Boulevard can sell for less. Homes on the rim of Switzer Canyon and the eastern canyons gain views and quiet but face slope and brush issues. Parking scarcity in denser blocks and the age of electrical, plumbing and foundations also shape buyer demand.

Valuation approach

Comparable sales are drawn from North Park blocks with similar walkability and lot characteristics, then from South Park, University Heights and Normal Heights when needed. Adjustments address living area, bedroom count, garage or off-street parking, ADU presence and permit status, and the degree to which original Craftsman details survive. Two- to four-unit properties are analyzed with both sales comparison and income data from leases. Owners should provide permits for additions and second units, any historic designation and Mills Act paperwork, rent rolls for income properties, and records of foundation, sewer lateral or electrical upgrades.

Local question

How does an appraiser handle an unpermitted rear unit on a North Park lot?

An unpermitted unit is described in the report but usually given limited contributory value, because a buyer would face legalization costs or removal. The appraiser may value the space as storage or a hobby room and compares with sales of homes that had similar unpermitted structures. Any city application, inspection records or plans for legalization should be provided, since a documented path to permit status can influence value.
