FAQ

Questions about ordering an appraisal

Report formats, the visit boundary, effective dates, timing and payment.

What types of appraisal reports are available?

Six report types are offered. No property visit is needed for desktop reports: Desktop ($449), Basic Desktop ($299) and Desktop 2-4 Unit ($549). Inspected reports include a physical viewing: Drive-By ($649), which observes the exterior from the street, Standard ($699), which includes an interior walk-through, and Standard 2-4 Unit ($799) for small multifamily buildings. The right choice depends on the purpose, whether condition is in question and who will read the report.

Where are inspected reports offered?

Standard and Drive-By reports are offered only in the 68 communities Brian Ward covers in person: 35 in Riverside County and 33 in San Diego County, Encinitas among them. Each community page on this site shows whether in-person service is available there. For property anywhere else in California, a desktop report is offered instead, built from records, listing data and photographs rather than a site visit.

Can an appraisal carry a past effective date, like a date of death?

Yes. Retrospective appraisals are a routine part of estate, trust, divorce and tax work. The report states an effective date in the past, and the analysis uses only sales and market information that would have been known around that date. Where the home has changed since then, the report describes it as it existed on the effective date, using records, photographs and the family's recollection of its condition at that time.

How long does an appraisal take?

Most reports are delivered within three or more business days of the order, depending on the report type, the complexity of the property and current workload. Inspected reports also depend on scheduling access to the home. Retrospective assignments and properties with few comparables can take longer. When a court date or filing deadline applies, mention it when ordering so the schedule can be confirmed in writing before the work starts.

When is payment due?

Payment is collected when the signed report is delivered rather than in advance. Cards, checks and bank transfers are accepted. The invoice can be addressed to an individual, an estate, a trust or a law firm, and it itemizes the report type and effective date for accounting and tax records.

What is the difference between a probate referee and a private appraisal?

In a California probate, the court names a probate referee, who values non-cash assets for filing the inventory and appraisal. That valuation serves the court's purpose but is not always prepared with a full sales comparison or a site visit. A private appraisal is ordered directly by the family, executor or attorney and documents the reasoning in detail. Families often order one to set stepped-up tax basis, to verify the referee's number, or to back a sale or buyout.

How do I order an appraisal?

Fill out the contact page's quote form or email the property address, the appraisal's purpose, the needed effective date and, for an inspected report, who will provide access. A written quote and expected delivery date are returned. Once the quote is accepted, the assignment is scheduled and any documents, such as a death certificate date or trust paperwork, can be sent over.

Do you do appraisals for home purchases financed by a bank?

No. Brian Ward does not accept assignments for financing transactions or work ordered through appraisal management companies. The practice is limited to private-party appraisals: estates, trusts, divorce, bankruptcy, property tax, insurance, pre-purchase and pre-listing decisions, family transfers and litigation. A buyer can still order a pre-purchase appraisal for personal decision-making, but it cannot be used to satisfy a bank's underwriting requirement.

Will the report be accepted by the IRS or a court?

A California Certified Residential Appraiser prepares each report following the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice, the benchmark expected by county assessors, the IRS and courts. Acceptance is never guaranteed by any appraiser, but a report that identifies its sources, explains each comparable and supports every adjustment gives a reviewer or opposing party little room to dismiss it. Testimony to support the report can be arranged when needed.

Is an appraisal useful for a Proposition 19 parent-child transfer?

Often, yes. Under Proposition 19, a child who inherits a parent's home can keep part of the prior assessed value only within a limit tied to the home's market value at transfer. A reliable market value at the date of transfer lets families and advisors project the reassessment and, where necessary, dispute the figure the assessor sets. The appraisal is dated to the transfer and analyzes sales from that period.

What does the appraiser need from me?

The essentials are the property address, the purpose of the report and the effective date. Helpful additions include recent photographs, a list of upgrades or repairs with approximate dates, any known defects, permits for additions and the contact person for access. For estates, the date of death and the attorney or executor's contact information are useful. For divorce, both parties' attorneys may need to receive the report.

Can both sides in a divorce share one appraisal?

Yes, and it frequently saves time and money. A jointly ordered report names both parties or both attorneys as intended users, so each side receives the same analysis. Neutral appraisals are common in mediation and collaborative divorce. If the matter is contested and each side wants its own expert, a separate report can be prepared for one party, and the appraiser can also review an opposing appraisal.

A site visit is part of the offer only where Brian works on the ground: 68 communities, 33 of them in San Diego County (Encinitas is one) and 35 in Riverside County. Drive-By and Standard reports, single-family or 2-4 unit, are booked for those addresses alone. Any other California property is valued with one of the three desktop formats.
